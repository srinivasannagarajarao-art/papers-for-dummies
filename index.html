Machine learning papers, translated into code you can break on purpose.

For engineers who ship models, not for researchers. 

def attention(query, keys, values):
    scores  = [dot(query, k) for k in keys]
    weights = softmax(scores)
    return sum(w * v for w, v in zip(weights, values))

That's the beating heart of Attention Is All You Need. Three lines. Every paper here gets the same treatment: the idea as a function you could set a breakpoint in, real output from running it, and a short list of things to break so you can watch what fails.

ai-ml/transformers/

  • Attention Is All You Need Vaswani et al., 2017

    Attention is a dictionary lookup with fuzzy keys — everything else is plumbing. Runnable NumPy, four demos that prove it by breaking it.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

  • RoFormer: Rotary Position Embedding Su et al., 2021

    Rotate q and k instead of adding a position vector, and the dot product only sees distance. Every claim is a printed number.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

  • GPT: Generative Pre-trained Transformers Radford et al., 2018 to Brown et al., 2020

    Decoder-only, next token, then scale. The generation loop is a while loop. A character-level GPT trained in your terminal.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

  • BERT: Deep Bidirectional Transformers Devlin et al., 2018

    Remove the causal mask, hide 15% of the tokens, guess them. Then bolt a head on per task. A tiny masked model, trained live.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

  • LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Models Touvron et al., 2023

    Not a new idea, a recipe: four deltas on the 2017 transformer and far more tokens than the scaling laws said. Parameter arithmetic included.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

  • Mixture of Experts: the Sparsely-Gated Layer Shazeer et al., 2017

    A load balancer in front of eight small networks. Eight times the parameters, twice the compute. Hot shards and dropped tokens, as in production.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

  • An Image Is Worth 16×16 Words (ViT) Dosovitskiy et al., 2020

    The whole paper is a reshape: cut the image into patches and it's a sentence. Shapes printed at every step.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

  • KV caching and the generation loop Folklore, and the papers that fixed it

    Why inference is affordable. The causal mask makes keys and values immutable, so you memoise them. Includes the cache-size arithmetic that decides what you can serve.

    English

ai-ml/fine-tuning/

  • LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation Hu et al., 2021

    A fine-tune is a diff on the weights, and the diff is low-rank. Store the patch, not the repo. Merge it for free at inference.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

  • Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning: the family Houlsby et al., 2019, and what followed

    One question five ways: where do you insert the small trainable thing? Adapters, prefixes, prompts, (IA)³ and LoRA, with parameter counts.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

ai-ml/alignment/

  • Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback Christiano et al., 2017

    You can't write the loss for "helpful", but you can ask which of two answers is better. Reward hacking, reproduced on purpose.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

  • InstructGPT: Following Instructions with Human Feedback Ouyang et al., 2022

    The three-stage recipe that turned a completion engine into an assistant. Each stage's gain in one table. This is ChatGPT's recipe.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

ai-ml/generative/

  • Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes (VAE) Kingma and Welling, 2013

    An autoencoder whose bottleneck is a distribution. The reparameterisation trick is moving the random number outside the function.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

  • Generative Adversarial Nets Goodfellow et al., 2014

    A forger and a detective train each other. Mode collapse and vanishing gradients, triggered deliberately on a toy problem.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

  • Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models Ho et al., 2020

    Learn to undo a noise-adding for-loop one step at a time. The forward process is a formula; the model is a one-step undo.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

ai-ml/retrieval/

  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation Lewis et al., 2020

    An open-book exam. The retriever is the index, the prompt is the page you slide across. The failures are retrieval bugs, not model bugs.

    English தமிழ் हिंदी

Who writes this

Srinivasan Nagaraja Rao. AI engineer. I ship code and models for a living, and I'm learning this field in public — including the parts where the maths scares me.

I'm not a researcher. I'm a programmer. When I read a machine learning paper, I don't want the proof — I want to know what it would look like as a function I could put a breakpoint in. So that's how I write these up.

Why "for dummies"

Because most paper explainers are written by people who are comfortable with the maths, aimed at people heading toward research. That's a real audience. It just isn't mine.

Mine is the engineer who has shipped models, done the MLOps, and still feels like a fraud when someone opens a paper. I'm writing these from inside that exact confusion, while I still remember what was confusing.

The method: translate the idea into code you already understand, then deliberately break it and watch what fails. You debug for a living. Reverse-engineering from broken behaviour is faster than re-reading section 3.2 for the fifth time.
